## Context

A growing garment operation relied on several connected tools for production planning, reporting and day-to-day coordination. Teams described intermittent connectivity and inconsistent access to current production information. The impact was difficult to quantify because incident records, system ownership and recovery procedures were fragmented.

The first priority was to understand where production depended on technology, identify the most consequential failure modes and avoid introducing change during critical operating windows.

## Options Considered

1. **Replace the production platform immediately.** This could address deeper application limitations, but carried high migration and adoption risk without reliable baseline data.
2. **Add monitoring only.** This was fast to start, but would report symptoms without resolving unclear ownership, weak recovery procedures or network dependencies.
3. **Stabilize and measure before larger change.** This staged approach paired service mapping and network checks with incident ownership, tested recovery steps and a later platform decision.

The third option was selected because it reduced near-term operational uncertainty while preserving the ability to make a better-informed investment decision later.

## Execution

### Discovery

Interviewed line supervisors, planners and support staff; mapped critical workflows and their systems; and reviewed available incident records. We agreed on a small set of production-critical services and operating windows where change needed additional approval.

### Analysis and design

Documented dependencies, ownership gaps and connectivity pain points. The team defined a baseline for incident impact, a prioritized risk register, monitoring requirements and acceptance criteria for the initial improvements.

### Build and test

In a controlled pilot, the team improved visibility for selected network paths, standardized incident categorization and documented escalation and recovery steps. Changes were tested with representative users before wider rollout, with rollback steps prepared for each change.

### Deploy and transition

Updates were scheduled around production commitments. IT and business owners reviewed the change checklist, confirmed support coverage and shared concise user communications. The service desk received updated runbooks and named escalation contacts.

### Operate and improve

The team reviewed incident trends and user feedback at regular checkpoints. Outstanding issues were added to the roadmap rather than bundled into an unscoped platform replacement.

## Challenges & Risks

- **Incomplete baseline:** historical incidents did not consistently describe production impact. We treated early measures as directional and improved data quality before making strong comparisons.
- **Change-window constraints:** factory work could not pause for convenient IT schedules. Stakeholders agreed on suitable windows and a clear rollback trigger.
- **Adoption and ownership:** process changes would not hold without business participation. Supervisors and service owners reviewed the workflow and escalation path before rollout.
- **Vendor and integration dependencies:** system behavior crossed organizational boundaries. We recorded assumptions and named vendor escalation routes instead of treating external dependencies as fully controlled.

## Lessons Learned

- Start with the production workflow and the consequence of interruption, not a preferred technology purchase.
- Baseline quality is part of the solution; label uncertainty rather than overstate early metrics.
- Recovery exercises and clear ownership create value even before a larger modernization decision.
- Bring line and business representatives into testing and communication planning from the start.
- Preserve the option to invest later by making early improvements measurable and reversible.
